1. History and Overview

DeFi protocols replace the market maker, the broker, and the credit officer with a public function that anyone can call and nobody can refuse. Every design in this document exists to answer one question: how do you price and lend an asset when there is no counterparty to negotiate with, no balance sheet to underwrite against, and no court to enforce a contract.

The answers are arithmetic. That is both the achievement and the attack surface.

1.1 Bancor Poses the Problem, Uniswap Solves It

The first on-chain market maker was Bancor in June 2017, and it was too complicated to win.

Bancor's design held reserves against a "smart token" with a configurable connector weight, and it charged gas for a formula involving fractional exponents. Uniswap v1, deployed on 2 November 2018 by Hayden Adams, threw out the exponents and kept one line of arithmetic: the product of the two reserves must not decrease. That constraint prices every trade, requires no order book, and costs a few thousand gas to check.

Uniswap v1 supported only ETH-to-token pairs and held about 80 million dollars at its peak on 14 February 2020. Uniswap v2, live on 6 May 2020, allowed arbitrary ERC-20 pairs, added a manipulation-resistant cumulative price accumulator, and introduced flash swaps. It peaked at 10.0 billion dollars on 29 April 2021 and still holds 1.01 billion as of 30 August 2026.

The lesson generalises. The winning primitive is not the most expressive one; it is the cheapest one that cannot be gamed.

1.2 Compound and Aave Pool the Credit

Lending arrived at the same time and took the opposite shape: a shared pool rather than a matched loan.

ETHLend, launched in 2017, matched individual lenders to individual borrowers. It failed for the reason peer-to-peer lending always fails: the order book was empty most of the time. Compound v1, live on 27 September 2018, pooled every lender's deposit into one reserve, let anyone borrow against collateral, and set the interest rate by formula rather than by negotiation. ETHLend rebranded to Aave in 2020 and copied the pooled design.

The pool solves the matching problem by making the lender's counterparty the pool itself. A depositor earns a blended rate and can withdraw whenever liquidity exists. A borrower pays a rate that rises as the pool empties. Nobody signs anything.

What the pool cannot solve is default. That is what collateral, oracles, and liquidation engines are for, and it is where almost all of the money is lost.

2. What a DeFi Protocol Is (and Is Not)

2.1 The Definition

A DeFi protocol is a set of smart contracts that holds pooled assets and executes a pricing or accounting rule that any address may invoke on identical terms. Four properties follow, and removing any one changes the category.

Custody sits in code, not in a firm. The contract holds the tokens. There is no omnibus account, no rehypothecation agreement, and no bankruptcy estate. When a contract is drained, there is nothing to claim against.

Prices come from a formula or a feed, never from a quote. An automated market maker computes the price from its own reserves. A lending pool computes the interest rate from its own utilisation and reads collateral prices from an oracle. No human sets a number.

Access is permissionless and identical. The same swap function serves a retail wallet and a 500-million-dollar arbitrage bot. There is no tiering, no KYC gate at the contract level, and no ability to refuse a caller.

Settlement is atomic. Either the whole transaction succeeds or the chain reverts to the prior state. This is what makes flash loans possible and what makes a multi-protocol exploit a single transaction rather than a campaign.

2.2 Two Misconceptions Worth Correcting

An AMM is not an order book, and "liquidity" does not mean the same thing. An order book holds resting limit orders that a taker crosses; depth is the sum of orders within a price band, and the maker chooses where to quote. An AMM holds inventory on a fixed curve; depth is a property of the curve and the reserves, and the "maker" cannot choose a price, only a range. The consequence is that an AMM always quotes, including when it should not. An order book maker pulls quotes ahead of news. A constant product pool sells into the news at the pre-news price until an arbitrageur has taken the difference. That difference has a name, loss-versus-rebalancing, and it is the structural cost of providing liquidity to an AMM.

Overcollateralised lending is not credit. A bank underwrites a borrower's ability to repay. Aave and Compound underwrite nothing about the borrower. They hold an asset worth more than the loan and sell it when the ratio slips. This is a pawnshop with an automated auctioneer, and it means DeFi lending cannot serve the purpose credit serves in the real economy: it cannot fund anyone who does not already have the money. What it does instead is offer leverage, tax deferral on an appreciating asset, and shorting. Those are real products. They are not credit.

2.3 What These Systems Are Not

Not decentralised in the way the name implies. Aave, Compound, Curve, and Balancer all have governance contracts that can change risk parameters, pause markets, and in some cases upgrade the implementation behind a proxy. A protocol with an upgradeable proxy and a multisig guardian has a trusted party; it has simply moved the trust from a licence to a key.

Not free of counterparty risk. The counterparty is the code, the oracle, the governance process, and every protocol whose token has been accepted as collateral. That list is longer than a bank's counterparty list, not shorter.

Not fee-free. A swap on Uniswap v2 costs 0.30% plus price impact plus gas plus whatever a searcher extracts by reordering the transaction. The all-in cost of a 40,000 dollar retail swap on a mid-sized pool routinely exceeds the cost of the same trade on a centralised exchange.

Not a settlement guarantee against economic loss. A liquidation engine guarantees that the protocol stays solvent, not that the borrower keeps anything. The borrower's downside is uncapped in the sense that matters: they can lose 105% of the debt value in collateral while still owing the remainder.

Not immutable in practice, and not upgradeable in principle. Uniswap v2 and v3 core contracts have no admin key and cannot be changed. Aave v3 and Compound v3 can be changed by governance within a timelock. Morpho Blue markets are immutable once created. Which model applies is the single most important thing to check before depositing, and it is rarely on the front page.

3.2 The Two Roles That Decide Whether a Protocol Survives

The arbitrageur is the price oracle of an AMM, and the LP pays for it. A constant product pool has no idea what ether is worth. It learns only when someone trades against a stale price and profits. That profit is the LP's loss, and it is not a bug: it is how the pool stays priced. Milionis, Moallemi, Roughgarden, and Zhang named the effect loss-versus-rebalancing in "Automated Market Making and Loss-Versus-Rebalancing" (arXiv:2208.06046, first posted 11 August 2022), and showed that for the Uniswap v2 ETH-USDC pool roughly 99.991% of LP return variance is simple market exposure, with fee income and arbitrage loss netting to a thin residual. LPs are paid a spread and charged an option premium. Whether the first exceeds the second is an empirical question with an uncomfortable answer.

The keeper is the solvency mechanism, and it only works when it is profitable. A lending protocol is solvent because bots race to buy discounted collateral the instant a position crosses the threshold. That race depends on three things being simultaneously true: the oracle updates, gas is affordable, and the liquidation bonus exceeds the cost of unwinding the seized asset. Break any one and the protocol accumulates bad debt. MakerDAO's Black Thursday on 12 March 2020 broke all three at once: ether fell 43% in 24 hours, gas spiked, the price feed lagged, and auction keepers stopped bidding. Auctions cleared at zero DAI, and the system ended the day with 5.67 million DAI of uncollateralised debt, covered by an emergency MKR auction on 28 March 2020.

The keeper is not a participant the protocol chooses. It is a participant the protocol prices into existence, and the price is the liquidation bonus.

4. The Constant Product Market Maker

4.1 The Invariant

A constant product market maker prices a trade by requiring that the product of its two reserves never falls. Everything else follows from that one sentence.

Let x be the reserve of token0 and y the reserve of token1. The invariant is:

x * y = k

A trader who sends dx of token0 receives the dy of token1 that leaves k unchanged:

(x + dx) * (y - dy) = x * y
dy = y * dx / (x + dx)

The marginal price is the ratio of reserves, P = y / x, and the pool quotes that price only for an infinitesimally small trade. Every real trade executes worse, because taking dy out raises x and lowers y, and the price is the ratio.

The fee enters as a haircut on the input, not as a separate transfer. Uniswap v2 charges 0.30% by multiplying the incoming amount by 997/1000 before applying the invariant. This is the check in UniswapV2Pair.swap, and it is the whole fee mechanism:

uint balance0Adjusted = balance0.mul(1000).sub(amount0In.mul(3));
uint balance1Adjusted = balance1.mul(1000).sub(amount1In.mul(3));
require(balance0Adjusted.mul(balance1Adjusted) >= uint(_reserve0).mul(_reserve1).mul(1000**2), 'UniswapV2: K');

The retained 0.30% stays in the reserves, so k grows with every trade. LP tokens are claims on a share of the reserves, so fees compound automatically in v2. They do not in v3 or v4, where fees accrue as a separate claimable balance.

The closed form for the output, which every router uses, is:

amountOut = (amountIn * 997 * reserveOut) / (reserveIn * 1000 + amountIn * 997)

4.2 What the Curve Guarantees

Three properties of x * y = k explain why it beat every more sophisticated design.

The pool can never be drained by trading. As x grows without bound, dy approaches y but never reaches it. Buying the last unit of a token costs infinite quantity of the other. No solvency check is needed, because the invariant is the solvency check.

Pricing is path-independent and stateless. The output depends only on the current reserves and the input. There is no queue, no priority, and no memory of who traded before. Two trades in either order leave the pool in the same state only if their combined effect is the same, which is precisely why ordering has value and MEV exists.

Gas is constant. A v2 swap costs roughly 100,000 gas end to end regardless of size. An order book match costs gas proportional to the number of orders crossed. On a chain where computation is the scarce resource, the constant-time algorithm wins even when it prices worse.

The cost of these properties is capital efficiency. A v2 pool spreads reserves across every price from zero to infinity. Uniswap's own documentation notes that the v2 DAI/USDC pair uses about 0.50% of its capital to serve trades between 0.99 and 1.01 dollars, where nearly all the volume occurs. The other 99.5% is inventory held against prices that will never print.

4.3 LP Tokens and the First Deposit

An LP deposit mints a fungible claim proportional to the share of reserves contributed. The first depositor sets the price by choosing the ratio, and mints sqrt(x * y) tokens minus a permanently burned minimum of 1,000 wei. That burn exists to prevent an attack in which the first depositor mints one wei of LP token, donates a large balance directly to the pair, and makes every subsequent deposit round down to zero shares.

That class of bug did not stay fixed. It reappeared as the "donation attack" or "inflation attack" in ERC-4626 vaults and in lending markets, and it is the root cause DefiLlama assigns to Euler's 197 million dollar loss on 13 March 2023, to Hundred Finance's 7.4 million on 15 April 2023, to Onyx's 2.1 million on 1 November 2023, and to Venus Core Pool's 3.7 million on 15 March 2026.

DefiLlama groups those four under one label, but the mechanisms differ. Hundred, Onyx, and Venus Core Pool are share-price inflation in the first-depositor sense: a donation makes later deposits round down. Euler is not. Its donateToReserves omitted a health check, which let the attacker push their own account underwater and self-liquidate at a discount, as section 18.3 sets out. Nothing rounded to zero and no share price was inflated.

One rounding direction, eight years, and the same fix rediscovered each time.

5. Slippage, Price Impact, and Order Flow

5.1 Three Costs That Get Called One Thing

The word "slippage" in a wallet interface conflates three distinct costs with different causes and different fixes.

Price impact is the movement caused by the trade itself against the curve. It is deterministic, computable before signing, and grows superlinearly with trade size relative to reserves. In the pool above, a 0.1 WETH sell costs 0.010% of impact on top of the 0.30% fee; a 100 WETH sell costs 9.091%.

The fee is the protocol's charge, fixed by the pool's tier. It is 0.30% on every v2 pool, one of 0.01%, 0.05%, 0.30% or 1.00% on v3, and any value from 0 to 100% in units of one hundredth of a basis point on v4.

Slippage proper is the difference between the quoted price at simulation time and the executed price, caused by other transactions landing first. It is not predictable. The user's only control is amountOutMin, the floor below which the transaction reverts, and that floor is exactly what an adversary optimises against.

5.3 Defences That Actually Work

Private order flow removes the mempool. Sending the transaction directly to a builder through a protected relay means the searcher never sees it before inclusion. This is now the default in several wallets and is the single most effective retail defence.

Batch auctions remove the ordering. CoW Protocol collects orders over a window, clears coincidences of wants internally at a uniform price, and routes only the residual to AMMs. Within a batch there is no ordering to sell.

Intents move the price risk to a filler. UniswapX and similar systems have the user sign a limit order with a decaying acceptable price rather than a swap; professional fillers compete to fill it and absorb the execution risk. The user's worst case is a fill at their stated limit, or no fill.

Dynamic fees tax the arbitrageur. A Uniswap v4 hook can raise the swap fee for the first transaction in a block, where nearly all arbitrage profit lands, and return the proceeds to LPs. This is the direct answer to loss-versus-rebalancing, and it exists only because v4 lets a pool run arbitrary code before a swap.

6. Impermanent Loss, Worked

6.1 The Definition and the Formula

Impermanent loss is the shortfall between holding an LP position and holding the same two assets in a wallet, measured at the same moment. It is caused by rebalancing, not by fees, not by price direction, and not by anything the LP did wrong.

The mechanism is one sentence. A constant product pool sells the asset that is rising and buys the asset that is falling, automatically, at every price on the way. That is a systematically wrong trade, and its cost is exactly computable.

Start from the invariant. With reserves x and y, k = x * y, and price P = y / x:

x = sqrt(k / P)        y = sqrt(k * P)
pool value V(P) = x*P + y = 2 * sqrt(k * P)

The LP deposited at price P0 and now observes P1. Write r = P1 / P0. Holding the original amounts would be worth x0*P1 + y0. Substituting and simplifying:

V_LP / V_HODL = 2 * sqrt(r) / (1 + r)

IL(r) = 2 * sqrt(r) / (1 + r) - 1

Three properties fall out. The result never exceeds zero, because the geometric mean never exceeds the arithmetic mean. It is symmetric in log r, so a doubling and a halving cost the same. And it depends only on the price ratio, not on the path, the volatility, or the elapsed time.

6.2 The Worked Example in Full

Deposit 1 WETH and 4,000 USDC at a price of 4,000, a position worth 8,000 USDC, with k = 4,000.

Ether doubles to 8,000. Arbitrageurs restore the pool price by buying WETH out of it, which leaves reserves of sqrt(4,000/8,000) = 0.70711 WETH and sqrt(4,000 * 8,000) = 5,656.85 USDC. The LP now owns 0.70711 WETH worth 5,656.85 USDC plus 5,656.85 USDC in cash, for a total of 11,313.71 USDC.

The wallet that did nothing holds 1 WETH worth 8,000 plus 4,000 USDC, for 12,000.

The gap is 686.29 USDC, or 5.7191% of the HODL value, matching 2*sqrt(2)/3 - 1 exactly. The LP still made money in dollar terms, up from 8,000 to 11,313.71. It made less money than doing nothing, which is the only comparison that matters.

Break-even requires cumulative fees above 686.29 USDC. In a v2 pool charging 0.30%, that is 228,763 USDC of volume attributable to this LP's share of the pool. Whether that volume arrives is the entire investment thesis.

6.3 Why "Impermanent" Is the Wrong Word

The name assumes the price returns. It says the loss reverses when r goes back to 1, which is true and useless, because there is no mechanism that makes prices mean-revert and the LP has no way to wait indefinitely.

The honest framing is that an LP has sold a straddle. The position is short volatility: it earns a fee stream and loses whenever the price moves in either direction. The fee is the option premium, the impermanent loss is the payoff to the option buyer, and the option buyer is the arbitrageur.

Loesch, Hindman, Richardson, and Welch measured this directly in "Impermanent Loss in Uniswap v3" (arXiv:2111.09192, 17 November 2021). Across 17 pools representing 43% of Uniswap v3 TVL, LPs earned 199.3 million dollars in fees and incurred 260.1 million dollars in impermanent loss. In aggregate those LPs would have been 60.8 million dollars better off holding.

That study covers a single period in a single market regime and should not be read as a permanent verdict. What it does establish is that fee income exceeding impermanent loss is a hypothesis to be tested per pool, not an assumption.

6.4 Concentration Multiplies Both Sides

Concentrating liquidity into a narrow range multiplies fee income and impermanent loss by roughly the same factor. A position with 20 times the capital efficiency earns about 20 times the fees while in range and suffers about 20 times the divergence loss for a given move inside the range.

The asymmetry is at the boundary. Once the price leaves the range, the position stops earning entirely and sits 100% in the losing asset. A full-range v2 position always earns something. A tight v3 position earns everything or nothing, and the transition is abrupt.

This makes v3 liquidity provision an active strategy rather than a deposit. It also explains why professional market makers now dominate the deepest v3 and v4 pools while retail LP share has fallen.

7. Concentrated Liquidity: Uniswap v3

7.1 The Change in One Sentence

Uniswap v3, live on 5 May 2021, lets a liquidity provider specify the price range over which their capital is active, which multiplies depth at the current price without adding capital.

The invariant becomes a virtual one. Inside the range [Pa, Pb], the position behaves exactly like a constant product pool holding virtual reserves large enough to produce the same depth. Outside the range, the position holds a single asset and earns nothing. The pool aggregates every position whose range spans the current price into one number, liquidity, and executes swaps against that.

The price is stored not as a ratio but as the square root of the ratio in Q64.96 fixed point, a 160-bit unsigned integer with 96 fractional bits, because the swap arithmetic is linear in sqrt(P) and quadratic in P. From TickMath.sol:

7.2 Ticks

Price space is discretised into ticks, where tick i corresponds to price 1.0001^i. One tick is one basis point of price, and the range covers prices from 2^-128 to 2^128.

A position's boundaries must fall on ticks that are multiples of the pool's tick spacing, which is bound to the fee tier by the factory constructor:

feeAmountTickSpacing[500] = 10;
feeAmountTickSpacing[3000] = 60;
feeAmountTickSpacing[10000] = 200;

The 0.01% tier with a tick spacing of 1 was added later by governance through enableFeeAmount. The pairing is deliberate: a cheap pool needs fine granularity because its users are chasing basis points, and an expensive pool needs coarse granularity because crossing an initialised tick costs gas and volatile pairs cross many.

7.3 Position Arithmetic, Worked

Take a WETH/USDC pool at 4,000 USDC per WETH in the 0.05% tier, and label WETH as token0 so the price reads directly. In the real mainnet pool USDC sorts below WETH and is token0, so the raw sqrtPriceX96 encodes WETH per USDC scaled by 10^12 for the decimal difference. The arithmetic below is identical either way; the labelling is chosen for legibility.

An LP opens a position from 3,600 to 4,400, a band of plus or minus 10%. With tick spacing 10, the nearest usable boundaries are tick 81,890 (price 3,599.6452) and tick 83,890 (price 4,396.5727).

Liquidity L relates to the two token amounts by:

amount0 = L * (1/sqrt(P) - 1/sqrt(Pb))      the token0 side, active above P
amount1 = L * (sqrt(P) - sqrt(Pa))          the token1 side, active below P

With sqrt(P) = 63.2456, sqrt(Pa) = 60, sqrt(Pb) = 66.3325, and a deposit of 10,000 USDC:

L       = 10,000 / (63.2456 - 60)          = 3,081.1388
amount0 = 3,081.1388 * (1/63.2456 - 1/66.3325) = 2.267167 WETH
position value = 2.267167 * 4,000 + 10,000  = 19,068.67 USDC

To provide the same depth L as a full-range v2 position requires L/sqrt(P) = 48.7171 WETH and L*sqrt(P) = 194,868.33 USDC, a position worth 389,736.66 USDC.

capital efficiency = 389,736.66 / 19,068.67 = 20.44x
closed form        = 1 / (1 - (Pa/Pb)^(1/4)) = 1 / (1 - 0.81818^0.25) = 20.44x

A plus or minus 10% band is 20 times more capital efficient than full range. A plus or minus 1% band around a stablecoin peg is roughly 200 times. That ratio is the entire product.

The callback is the structural difference from v2. The pool pays out first, calls back into the caller, and then checks that its balance rose. That makes every v3 swap a flash swap by construction: a caller may take the output, do anything, and pay at the end of the same transaction.

7.5 Fee Accounting Without Loops

The pool cannot iterate over positions, so it tracks fees per unit of liquidity globally and computes each position's share by difference.

feeGrowthGlobal0X128 accumulates total fees per unit of liquidity, ever, as a Q128.128 number. Each tick stores feeGrowthOutside0X128, the fees accrued on the far side of that tick. A position's earned fees are:

feeGrowthInside = feeGrowthGlobal - feeGrowthOutside(lower) - feeGrowthOutside(upper)
owed = L * (feeGrowthInside_now - feeGrowthInside_at_last_update) / 2^128

Both accumulators are allowed to overflow, and the subtraction is done in unsigned arithmetic so the wrap cancels. This is deliberate and correct, and it is one of the parts of v3 that reviewers most often flag as a bug.

Because fees are tracked separately rather than added to reserves, v3 fees do not compound. An LP must call collect and redeposit. Uniswap v4 changes this: accrued fees act as a credit when modifying liquidity, so increasing a position converts fee revenue into liquidity automatically.

7.6 The v3 Oracle

Every v3 pool doubles as a time-weighted average price oracle, and the design is the reason TWAPs became the standard defence against spot manipulation.

Each pool stores a ring buffer of observations:

struct Observation {
    uint32 blockTimestamp;
    int56 tickCumulative;
    uint160 secondsPerLiquidityCumulativeX128;
    bool initialized;
}

tickCumulative is the running sum of the active tick multiplied by elapsed seconds. The arithmetic mean tick over any interval is the difference of two accumulator readings divided by the elapsed time, which corresponds to a geometric mean price. The buffer starts at length 1 and any caller may pay to grow it up to 65,535 slots, which on Ethereum covers roughly nine days.

observe(uint32[] secondsAgos) returns interpolated readings at arbitrary points, so a consumer never has to search the array. A 30-minute TWAP is two storage reads and a subtraction.

Uniswap v4 removes the built-in oracle. A pool that wants one attaches a hook. That is a real regression for integrators who relied on every pool being an oracle, and a real improvement for pools that never needed one and were paying the storage cost anyway.

8. Uniswap v4: The Singleton and Hooks

8.1 Four Changes

Uniswap v4 went live at the end of January 2025 and reached 0.99 billion dollars of TVL and 25.16 billion dollars of 30-day volume by 30 August 2026, overtaking v3 on both volume and fee revenue. The concentrated liquidity mathematics is unchanged. Everything around it is different.

One contract holds every pool. PoolManager.sol stores all pool state in mappings keyed by a PoolId, the hash of a PoolKey. Creating a pool is a state write rather than a CREATE2 deployment, which cuts pool creation from roughly 4.5 million gas to tens of thousands, the 99% reduction Uniswap states in the v4 announcement.

struct PoolKey {
    Currency currency0;   // lower address, sorted numerically; address(0) means native ETH
    Currency currency1;
    uint24 fee;           // capped at 1_000_000; 0x800000 marks a dynamic-fee pool
    int24 tickSpacing;
    IHooks hooks;
}

Balances net inside a transaction. Flash accounting records every balance change as a signed delta in EIP-1153 transient storage rather than transferring tokens at each step. A caller invokes unlock, receives an unlockCallback, performs any sequence of swap, modifyLiquidity, donate, take, settle, mint, burn and sync calls, and the manager verifies at the end that every delta is zero. A three-hop swap moves tokens twice instead of six times.

Fees are arbitrary and can be dynamic. A pool creator sets any fee from 0 to 100% in hundredths of a basis point. Setting the fee to 0x800000 marks the pool as dynamic, after which the attached hook sets the fee per swap by returning an override from beforeSwap or by calling updateDynamicLPFee.

Native ETH is a first-class currency. Currency of address(0) means ether. No wrapping, no WETH9 approval, no extra transfer.

The cost is that a hook's permissions are frozen at deployment and visible in its address. That is a feature for auditors and a constraint for developers.

8.3 What Hooks Break

Hooks move code that used to be immutable into contracts that are not. Three consequences follow, and all three are now standard review items.

A hook can revert. beforeSwap runs before the swap, and a malicious or buggy hook can block withdrawals, block liquidity removal, or block only certain callers. The liquidity modification hooks are split into four separate callbacks precisely so that a pool can permit a hook to gate additions without letting it gate removals.

A hook can take value. With BEFORE_SWAP_RETURNS_DELTA_FLAG set, a hook returns a BeforeSwapDelta that alters the amounts. That is how custom curves and hook fees work, and it is also how a hook could route the entire swap to itself.

A hook is not audited because the pool is. Uniswap v4 core is heavily audited. A hook written last week is not. The Uniswap interface does not route to arbitrary hooks by default, which limits the blast radius, but a user who interacts directly with a hooked pool inherits its code.

9. The Other Curves: StableSwap, Weighted, Dynamic

9.1 StableSwap

Curve's StableSwap invariant, introduced in February 2020, interpolates between a constant sum and a constant product so that assets expected to trade near parity get near-zero slippage in the middle and constant-product protection at the edges.

For n coins with balances x_i, an amplification coefficient A, and invariant D:

A * n^n * sum(x_i) + D = A * D * n^n + D^(n+1) / (n^n * prod(x_i))

Set A to zero and the equation collapses to the constant product prod(x_i) = (D/n)^n. Let A grow without bound and it collapses to the constant sum sum(x_i) = D. Real pools sit between: A for a well-behaved stablecoin trio is in the low hundreds.

There is no closed form for D, so the contract solves it by Newton iteration, capped at 255 rounds and exiting when successive estimates differ by one wei:

D = S
Ann = amp * N_COINS
for _i in range(255):
    D_P = D
    for _x in xp:
        D_P = D_P * D / (_x * N_COINS)
    Dprev = D
    D = (Ann * S + D_P * N_COINS) * D / ((Ann - 1) * D + (N_COINS + 1) * D_P)

The comment in the source next to the inner division reads "If division by 0, this will be borked: only withdrawal will work. And that is good," which is an accurate summary of the failure mode when a pool balance reaches zero.

StableSwap's weakness is the assumption in its name. When a constituent depegs, the curve's flatness means the pool absorbs the bad asset at near-par until the balance is heavily skewed. That is what happened to Curve pools holding UST in May 2022 and to the stETH pool during the June 2022 discount.

9.2 Weighted Pools

Balancer generalises the constant product to n assets with arbitrary weights:

prod(B_i ^ W_i) = V,   where sum(W_i) = 1

Two assets at 50/50 reduce to x * y = k. An 80/20 pool holds four times as much of the first asset by value and suffers roughly two thirds the impermanent loss of a 50/50 pool for the same price move, which is why protocols use 80/20 pools of their own token against ether for treasury liquidity. The value ratio is r^w / (w*r + 1 - w), so divergence loss scales with w * (1 - w), which is 0.16 against 0.25 for small moves. A doubling costs an 80/20 pool 3.272% against the 50/50 pool's 5.719%, a ratio of 0.57.

Balancer's implementation cost is arithmetic. Weighted math needs pow with fractional exponents, which needs high-precision fixed-point routines, which need rounding decisions at every step. That is exactly where the 3 November 2025 exploit lived.

10. Overcollateralised Lending Pools

10.1 The Shape of the Thing

A lending pool is one contract per market holding every supplier's deposit, against which any address may borrow so long as it posts collateral worth more than the loan. Four numbers govern each asset, and every one of them is a governance parameter.

Loan-to-value (LTV) caps how much can be borrowed against an asset at the moment of borrowing. WETH on Aave v3 Ethereum is 80.5% on 30 August 2026.

Liquidation threshold (LT) is the higher ratio at which the position becomes liquidatable. WETH is 83%. The gap between LTV and LT is the buffer that lets a borrower open a position without being immediately liquidatable by a one-tick price move.

Liquidation bonus is the discount a liquidator receives on seized collateral. WETH is 5%, USDC and USDT are 4.5%, and long-tail assets run to 10%.

Reserve factor is the share of borrower interest diverted to the protocol treasury instead of suppliers. USDC is 10%, WETH is 15%, WBTC is 50%.

10.2 Indexed Balances

Neither Aave nor Compound stores a user's balance. Both store a scaled amount and a global index, and compute the balance on read.

Aave mints aTokens whose balanceOf is scaledBalance * liquidityIndex / RAY, where RAY is 10^27. Debt works the same way against variableBorrowIndex. Interest accrual is therefore a single index update per reserve per block touched, not a loop over users. A supplier's aToken balance rises continuously without any transfer event, which is why aTokens confuse accounting software and why Aave also publishes a static, non-rebasing wrapper.

Compound v2 takes the other approach. A cToken has a fixed balance and a rising exchangeRate, so 100 cUSDC is always 100 cUSDC and simply redeems for more USDC over time. Compound v3 uses a third form, a principal value stored as a signed integer where positive means supply and negative means borrow, with separate supply and borrow indices.

All three encode the same fact. Interest is a change in an index, and a balance is a claim on an index.

10.3 Isolation, E-Mode, and Caps

Aave v3 added three containment features in March 2022, and each exists because of a specific prior loss.

Supply and borrow caps bound the absolute exposure to any reserve. A cap prevents an attacker from supplying an unlimited amount of a manipulable token as collateral, which is the precondition for most oracle attacks.

Isolation mode lets governance list a risky asset as collateral that can only borrow approved stablecoins, up to a debt ceiling denominated in dollars. A borrower in isolation mode cannot use any other asset as collateral simultaneously.

Efficiency mode (e-mode) raises LTV and liquidation threshold for positions where collateral and debt are correlated. The Aave v3 Ethereum ETH-correlated category runs collateral at a 93% LTV and a 95% liquidation threshold with a 1% liquidation bonus, precisely because the intended use is looping wstETH against WETH, where the price ratio moves within a narrow band. The BTC-pair categories run 84% and 86% with a 3% bonus, and the sUSDe stablecoin category 90% and 92% with a 4% bonus.

E-mode is the feature that made liquid staking leverage a mass-market product, and it is also the feature that concentrates the protocol's risk into one correlation assumption. If wstETH ever depegs from ETH by more than the e-mode buffer, the liquidation engine has to unwind billions against a market that is by definition dislocated.

10.4 Aave v4 and the Hub-and-Spoke Model

Aave v4 went live at the end of March 2026 and holds 356 million dollars on 30 August 2026, a rounding error against v3's 17.36 billion. The architecture is a bet on modularity.

A Liquidity Hub holds the pooled assets and the system-wide accounting. Spokes are separate modules that implement particular borrowing use cases, each granted a credit line for borrowing from the Hub and a debit line for supplying into it. Users interact with Spokes; the Hub enforces the caps.

The point is that governance can add a market, a collateral type, or an entire risk profile by deploying a new Spoke, without migrating liquidity. Aave v3 required a fresh Pool deployment and a liquidity migration for every isolated market, which is why AaveV3EthereumLido, AaveV3EthereumEtherFi, and AaveV3EthereumHorizon exist as separate deployments with separately fragmented reserves.

Two v4 mechanisms change the risk model rather than the plumbing. A user risk premium adds a per-borrower surcharge to the shared borrow rate, weighted by the quality of the collateral backing that debt, and recalculated as the position changes. And liquidations replace the fixed close factor with a target health factor set per Spoke, so a liquidator repays only enough to restore the position to that target, with a variable liquidation bonus that rises as health falls. Both are attempts to price risk at the position level rather than the asset level.

11. Interest Rate Curves and Utilisation

11.1 Utilisation Is the Only Input

A lending pool sets its interest rate from one number: the fraction of supplied assets currently borrowed.

U = totalDebt / (availableLiquidity + totalDebt)

The rate must rise with U for a mechanical reason, not an economic one. When U reaches 1 the pool is empty and no supplier can withdraw. The rate curve exists to make that outcome expensive enough that it does not persist: as the pool empties, borrowing gets dearer and supplying gets richer, and both sides respond.

Aave computes the borrow rate as a two-segment piecewise linear function with a kink at optimalUsageRatio:

if (borrowUsageRatio > optimalUsageRatio) {
    excessBorrowUsageRatio = (borrowUsageRatio - optimalUsageRatio).rayDiv(RAY - optimalUsageRatio);
    currentVariableBorrowRate += variableRateSlope1 + variableRateSlope2.rayMul(excessBorrowUsageRatio);
} else {
    currentVariableBorrowRate += variableRateSlope1.rayMul(borrowUsageRatio).rayDiv(optimalUsageRatio);
}

currentLiquidityRate = currentVariableBorrowRate
    .rayMul(supplyUsageRatio)
    .percentMul(PERCENTAGE_FACTOR - reserveFactor);

In plain arithmetic:

borrowRate = base + slope1 * U / Uopt                              for U <= Uopt
borrowRate = base + slope1 + slope2 * (U - Uopt) / (1 - Uopt)      for U >  Uopt

supplyRate = borrowRate * U * (1 - reserveFactor)

The supply rate is the borrow rate diluted by the fraction actually lent out and net of the treasury's cut. A supplier in a pool at 50% utilisation earns at most half the borrow rate, before the reserve factor.

Aave bounds the parameters at deployment: optimalUsageRatio between 1% and 99%, variableRateSlope1 no greater than variableRateSlope2, and the sum base + slope1 + slope2 capped at 1000% by MAX_BORROW_RATE.

11.3 Compound's Variants

Compound v2 uses the same shape with different names. BaseJumpRateModelV2 stores per-block rates and a kink:

function utilizationRate(uint cash, uint borrows, uint reserves) public pure returns (uint) {
    if (borrows == 0) return 0;
    return borrows * BASE / (cash + borrows - reserves);
}

function getBorrowRateInternal(uint cash, uint borrows, uint reserves) internal view returns (uint) {
    uint util = utilizationRate(cash, borrows, reserves);
    if (util <= kink) {
        return ((util * multiplierPerBlock) / BASE) + baseRatePerBlock;
    } else {
        uint normalRate = ((kink * multiplierPerBlock) / BASE) + baseRatePerBlock;
        uint excessUtil = util - kink;
        return ((excessUtil * jumpMultiplierPerBlock) / BASE) + normalRate;
    }
}

Note uint public constant blocksPerYear = 2102400. That constant assumes 15-second blocks. Ethereum has produced 12-second slots since the Merge in September 2022, roughly 2,628,000 slots a year. A model that converts an annual rate to a per-block rate using 2,102,400 and is then applied 2,628,000 times a year overshoots the intended annual rate by about 25%. Compound v3 avoids the problem entirely by expressing rates per second.

Compound v3 also decouples the two curves. It stores supplyKink and borrowKink separately with independent base rates and slopes, so the spread between what borrowers pay and what suppliers earn is set directly rather than falling out of the reserve factor.

11.4 Where the Model Breaks

Rates are set by supply and demand for one asset, not by the borrower's risk. Every USDC borrower on Aave v3 pays the same rate whether they posted WETH or a thin governance token. That is a deliberate simplification, and it means the pool cross-subsidises risky collateral. Aave v4's user risk premium is the first serious attempt to unwind it.

A pool at 100% utilisation is a bank run in slow motion. Suppliers cannot withdraw, and the only relief is a borrower repaying or a new supplier arriving to earn the elevated rate. The curve makes both attractive, and usually that is enough. When the reason for 100% utilisation is that the borrowed asset has become worthless, it is not.

The utilisation signal can be manipulated. A borrower who wants to spike a rate can borrow the free liquidity, which is exactly the setup used against protocols that read a lending rate as an oracle input. The reverse is also true: a large supply deposit right before an interest accrual can dilute a rate. Neither attack works well against Aave or Compound, where rates update on every state-changing interaction, but both work against integrations that snapshot a rate.

12. Liquidation Engines, Keepers, and Bonuses

12.1 The Health Factor

Every overcollateralised protocol reduces a position to one scalar and compares it to 1.

healthFactor = sum(collateral_i * price_i * liquidationThreshold_i) / sum(debt_j * price_j)

Below 1, anyone may call liquidationCall and repay part of the debt in exchange for collateral at a discount. Aave states the threshold as a constant and the codebase treats it as absolute:

uint256 public constant HEALTH_FACTOR_LIQUIDATION_THRESHOLD = 1e18;

The health factor is not a probability, a credit score, or a risk measure. It is a ratio of two oracle-derived sums, and it is only as good as the oracle underneath it. Every large lending loss in this document is a case where the numerator was wrong.

12.2 The Close Factor

The close factor caps how much of a debt a single liquidation may repay. Aave v3 sets it in LiquidationLogic.sol:

uint256 internal constant DEFAULT_LIQUIDATION_CLOSE_FACTOR = 0.5e4;      // 50%
uint256 public constant CLOSE_FACTOR_HF_THRESHOLD = 0.95e18;             // HF 0.95
uint256 public constant MIN_BASE_MAX_CLOSE_FACTOR_THRESHOLD = 2000e8;    // $2,000
uint256 public constant MIN_LEFTOVER_BASE = MIN_BASE_MAX_CLOSE_FACTOR_THRESHOLD / 2;

The rule reads as three conditions. A liquidator may repay 50% of the debt when the health factor is above 0.95 and both the collateral and the debt in that reserve exceed 2,000 dollars. If the health factor is at or below 0.95, or either leg is under 2,000 dollars, the whole debt becomes liquidatable. And a partial liquidation must leave at least 1,000 dollars on both sides, so liquidators cannot save gas by leaving dust that nobody will ever clean up.

Compound v2 sets the same idea as a governance parameter bounded in the Comptroller between 5% and 90%:

uint internal constant closeFactorMinMantissa = 0.05e18;
uint internal constant closeFactorMaxMantissa = 0.9e18;
uint internal constant collateralFactorMaxMantissa = 0.9e18;

The close factor exists to protect the borrower from being fully liquidated on a momentary dip. It also caps the liquidator's revenue per transaction, which is why deeply underwater positions are liquidated in a sequence of transactions rather than one.

12.3 The Bonus and Who Pays It

The liquidation bonus is a discount on seized collateral, and the borrower pays it out of equity. Compound v2 computes the seizure directly:

seizeAmount = actualRepayAmount * liquidationIncentive * priceBorrowed / priceCollateral
seizeTokens = seizeAmount / exchangeRate

Aave v3 applies the reserve's liquidationBonus and then splits it, sending a governance-configured liquidationProtocolFee share of the bonus to the treasury and the remainder to the liquidator. On Aave v3 Ethereum that share is 10% for WETH and 20% for USDC. The bonus is set per asset and scales with how hard the asset is to sell: 4.5% for USDC and USDT, 5% for WETH and WBTC, 7.5% for cbBTC and rETH, 8.5% for USDe and sUSDe, 10% for eBTC, UNI, KNC, and FXS.

That gradient is the whole risk model in one column. An asset with a 10% bonus is one the protocol expects to move 10% against it during an unwind.

Bad debt appears when collateral value falls below debt value faster than keepers can act. Three mechanisms produce that.

The gap move. Between a liquidation price of 2,891.57 and a collateral-equals-debt price of 2,400, there is a 17% band in which the position is liquidatable but still solvent. A single candle through that band leaves the protocol holding collateral worth less than the loan.

The illiquid collateral. If the seized asset cannot be sold for the marked price, the bonus is illusory and no keeper acts. 13. Oracles and the Attacks They Enable

13.1 The Oracle Problem

A lending protocol needs to know what collateral is worth, and a blockchain knows only what is on it. Every design for closing that gap trades manipulation resistance against latency, and every large DeFi lending loss lives somewhere on that trade-off.

Oracle manipulation accounts for 963 million dollars across 154 recorded incidents, of which 126 are specifically spot price manipulation. That is the seventh-largest loss category by total, behind key compromise, social engineering, access control, token and share accounting, bridges and front ends, and the largest that is purely a pricing-design failure rather than an operational or cross-chain one.

13.2 Chainlink, Concretely

A Chainlink data feed is a set of independent node operators running off-chain reporting, agreeing on a median, and writing one transmission to an aggregator contract. A new round starts when either condition fires:

  • Deviation threshold: an operator observes the off-chain value differing from the on-chain value by more than a configured percentage.
  • Heartbeat: a configured interval elapses since the last update.

Consumers read latestRoundData() on the AggregatorV3Interface, which returns roundId, answer, startedAt, updatedAt, and answeredInRound. Two checks separate a correct integration from a vulnerable one. The consumer must verify updatedAt is recent enough for its own risk tolerance, and must handle the case where the feed is not updating at all.

Failing to check staleness has a body count. DefiLlama records 8 incidents classified as stale oracle price, including Venus Protocol's 11.2 million dollar loss on 12 May 2022, and 18 further incidents classified as oracle misconfiguration, including Aave v3's 0.86 million dollar loss on 12 March 2026.

The deviation threshold is also the attack budget. A feed that updates on a 0.5% deviation guarantees only that the on-chain price is within 0.5% of the off-chain consensus, plus whatever moved since. A protocol that liquidates at a 5% buffer is safe against that. A protocol that arbitrages against a 0.1% edge is not.

13.5 What Actually Defends

Never price collateral from a pool the borrower can trade into. This single rule eliminates the 126 spot-price incidents.

Use a TWAP whose window exceeds the cost of holding a manipulated price. A 30-minute Uniswap v3 TWAP requires the attacker to hold the wrong price for 150 Ethereum blocks while every arbitrageur trades against them. On a chain with sub-second blocks and a single sequencer, that guarantee weakens sharply.

Cross-check two independent sources and halt on divergence. Aave, Compound, and Morpho vaults all now use circuit breakers that pause a market when a feed moves more than a configured amount in one update.

Cap the exposure, not just the price. Supply caps and debt ceilings bound the loss even when the price is wrong. If a token's supply cap is 5 million dollars, a perfect oracle attack against it cannot cost more than 5 million dollars.

Price wrappers by redemption, not by market. wstETH should be priced as stETH per wstETH from the Lido contract multiplied by an ETH/USD feed, not as a wstETH/ETH market price. The redemption rate cannot be moved by trading.

14. Flash Loans

14.1 The Mechanism

A flash loan is an uncollateralised loan that must be repaid inside the transaction that took it. It exists because a blockchain transaction is atomic: if the repayment check fails at the end, every state change including the loan itself is reverted, so the lender is never at risk.

The consequence is that creditworthiness stops being a property of the borrower. There is nothing to underwrite, because there is no state in which the loan is outstanding and unpaid.

Aave exposes two entry points on Pool.sol:

function flashLoan(
    address receiverAddress,
    address[] calldata assets,
    uint256[] calldata amounts,
    uint256[] calldata interestRateModes,
    address onBehalfOf,
    bytes calldata params,
    uint16 referralCode
) public virtual override;

function flashLoanSimple(
    address receiverAddress,
    address asset,
    uint256 amount,
    bytes calldata params,
    uint16 referralCode
) public virtual override;

flashLoan borrows several assets at once and, if interestRateModes is nonzero for an asset, may leave the borrow open as an ordinary collateralised debt rather than repaying it. flashLoanSimple borrows one asset, waives nothing, and must be repaid. The receiving contract implements executeOperation and must have approved the Pool to pull amount + premium.

The fee is 0.05% at deployment and adjustable by governance through FLASHLOAN_PREMIUM_TOTAL. The whole premium goes to the treasury. FLASHLOAN_PREMIUM_TO_PROTOCOL is hardcoded to 100_00 in the current Pool, and FlashLoanLogic._handleFlashLoanRepayment credits the full amount to reserve.accruedToTreasury, so suppliers receive none of it. In the original 2022 v3.0 deployment the protocol share was zero and the premium accrued to suppliers through the liquidity index. Addresses granted the FLASH_BORROWER role by the ACL Manager pay nothing.

In all three cases the caller holds the protocol's assets mid-transaction. 14.2 The Legitimate Uses

Flash loans are load-bearing infrastructure, not a curiosity. Four uses carry real volume.

Liquidations. As shown in section 12, a keeper repays 120,000 USDC without owning it. This is the largest legitimate use by count and the reason liquidation is competitive.

Collateral swaps and debt refinancing. A borrower with WETH collateral and USDC debt on Aave who wants WBTC collateral flash-borrows USDC, repays the debt, withdraws the WETH, swaps to WBTC, redeposits, reborrows, and repays. Aave ships this as a product feature.

Deleveraging in one transaction. A position too large to unwind incrementally can be closed atomically without ever being undercollateralised in between.

Arbitrage. Price differences between venues get closed by whoever can act, not by whoever has capital, which tightens spreads for everyone.

14.3 What Flash Loans Actually Change About Attacks

The common claim is that flash loans cause exploits. The accurate claim is narrower and more useful: flash loans remove capital as a barrier, and therefore remove capital as a control.

Before flash loans, a protocol could survive a design flaw if exploiting it required 500 million dollars, because the set of entities holding 500 million dollars and willing to spend it on a crime is small and identifiable. After flash loans, that set is everyone with a laptop.

The distinction matters for defence. A vulnerability that flash loans enable is a vulnerability that a well-capitalised adversary always had. Fixing it means fixing the design, not banning the loan. DefiLlama labels only 4 incidents totalling 182.1 million dollars as flash-loan attacks proper, all of them flash-loan governance attacks. The other hundreds of incidents where a flash loan appears in the transaction trace are classified by their actual root cause, which is where they belong.

Two categories of attack are genuinely created rather than merely enabled.

Governance capture by borrowed votes. Beanstalk on 17 April 2022 is the archetype. The attacker flash-borrowed 350 million DAI, 500 million USDC, and 150 million USDT from Aave, plus 32 million BEAN from Uniswap v2 and 11.6 million LUSD from SushiSwap, converted them into Beanstalk's LP-based governance tokens, and executed a proposal called BIP-18 that transferred the protocol's assets to their own address. Total extracted: 181 million dollars, of which 76 million was profit after repaying the loans. The proposal had been submitted a day earlier so that the governance delay had already elapsed; the emergencyCommit path then allowed immediate execution once the vote threshold was met.

The fix is structural and now standard: snapshot voting power at a block before the proposal, and never at execution time.

Within-block state manipulation. Any protocol that reads a value derivable from balances it does not control, in the same transaction as it acts on that value, is exploitable at flash-loan scale. That includes spot prices, vault share prices, utilisation rates, and LP token valuations.

14.4 The Defences

Snapshot governance power historically. Compound's getPriorVotes reads a checkpointed balance at a past block. Borrowed tokens confer no votes.

Read time-weighted or externally attested prices. A TWAP or a Chainlink feed cannot be moved inside one transaction.

Block same-block deposit and withdrawal. Many vaults now record the block of the last deposit per address and reject a withdrawal in the same block, which defeats a large class of share-price manipulation.

Charge for the loan. A 0.05% premium on 500 million dollars is 250,000 dollars, which is a meaningful floor under any attack that needs a large notional and returns a thin margin.

15. Composability and the Risk It Compounds

15.1 What Composability Buys

Composability is the property that any contract may call any other contract's public functions without permission, in the same transaction, with the result of one feeding the input of the next. It is the reason a single transaction can flash-borrow, swap, supply, borrow, and liquidate.

The economic gain is real. A single deposit of ether can be staked into stETH, wrapped to wstETH, supplied to Aave, borrowed against for USDC, and that USDC supplied to a Morpho vault which itself lends against the same wstETH. Every layer earns a spread on the same underlying asset. Nobody had to ask anybody's permission to build any of it.

The risk is that every layer inherits every assumption of every layer below.

15.2 Four Ways Composition Compounds Risk

Correlated liquidation cascades. Leveraged loops of a liquid staking token against ether are profitable precisely because the two prices track each other. When they stop tracking, every looped position becomes liquidatable at once, and the liquidations sell the asset that is already at a discount. The stETH discount of June 2022 produced exactly this: forced unwinds of loops on Aave drove stETH further below ether, which triggered more unwinds.

Shared code with unshared audits. The Balancer v2 exploit on 3 November 2025 lost 128.0 million dollars to a unidirectional rounding flaw in the _upscale function, which always rounded down instead of rounding in the direction that favours the protocol. The flaw was in composable stable pools holding liquid staking derivatives. Twenty-seven Balancer v2 forks inherited the same code, and copycat attacks began within 53 minutes across Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base, Optimism, Polygon, Sonic, and Berachain, where an emergency hard fork recovered 12.86 million dollars.

Shared toolchains. The Curve incident of 30 July 2023 was not a Curve bug. Vyper versions 0.2.15, 0.2.16, and 0.3.0 misaligned storage slots between add_liquidity and remove_liquidity, silently disabling the reentrancy lock on any pool holding native ether. The bug had been present since 2021 and fixed in 0.3.1 without anyone realising what it broke. Four unrelated protocols lost funds in a single day: Curve's CRV/ETH pool for 24.2 million dollars, Alchemix's alETH/ETH for 20.5 million, JPEG'd's pETH/ETH for 11.5 million, and Metronome's msETH/ETH for 1.6 million. DefiLlama records the aggregate at 61.7 million, with roughly 17 million recovered by whitehats.

Derived prices on derived prices. An LP token's value is computed from pool reserves. A vault share's value is computed from the vault's holdings, which may be LP tokens. A lending market that accepts vault shares as collateral is three derivations away from any price anyone quoted. Warp Finance in December 2020 and Cheese Bank in November 2020 both died at exactly this depth.

15.3 The Uncomfortable Arithmetic

Compose n independent components each with an annual failure probability p, and the system's survival probability is (1-p)^n. At p = 1% and n = 5, the composed system has a 4.9% chance of failing at some point in a given year. At n = 10, 9.6%.

The assumption of independence is generous. The Curve, Balancer, and Vyper cases all show correlated failure, where one root cause hits many components at once. Correlated failure makes the composed number worse than the independent calculation, not better.

The practical rule is to count the dependencies before depositing, and to treat every "audited" badge as covering one layer only.

16. Yield Farming and Governance Tokens

16.1 Compound Invents It, Accidentally

On 15 June 2020, Compound activated Proposal 007 and began distributing COMP to suppliers and borrowers of every market, at a fixed rate of 0.5 COMP per Ethereum block split between the two sides in proportion to each market's borrowing. COMP has a fixed total supply of 10,000,000 tokens, hardcoded in Comp.sol.

The intent was to decentralise governance. The effect was to create an instrument whose yield was denominated in a token whose price was set by the demand created by the yield.

Within days, users discovered that borrowing an asset could be profitable if the COMP earned exceeded the interest paid. Capital flowed into whichever market maximised COMP per dollar rather than whichever market needed liquidity. Compound's TVL rose from roughly 100 million dollars to over 600 million in a month, and every subsequent protocol copied the design.

16.2 The Vampire Attack

SushiSwap launched on 28 August 2020 as a fork of Uniswap v2 with one addition: SUSHI tokens paid to anyone who staked Uniswap LP tokens. Its MasterChef contract sets startBlock to Ethereum block 10,750,000, mined at 15:31 UTC that day. Twelve days later, on 9 September 2020, SushiSwap migrated the underlying liquidity into its own contracts.

It worked. Uniswap responded on 16 September 2020 by launching UNI with a genesis supply of 1,000,000,000 tokens allocated 60% to the community, 21.27% to the team, 18.04% to investors, and 0.69% to advisors, all vesting over four years, with 2% perpetual annual inflation thereafter. The retroactive airdrop distributed 15% of supply based on a 1 September 2020 snapshot: 400 UNI to every address that had ever called a Uniswap v1 or v2 contract, roughly 49 million UNI to liquidity providers weighted by duration, and 1,000 UNI to holders of the Unisocks token. Four pools received 5 million UNI each in liquidity mining between 18 September and 17 November 2020.

Uniswap's TVL peaked at 10.00 billion dollars on 29 April 2021. SushiSwap's peaked at 7.04 billion on 9 November 2021 and stands at 0.04 billion on 30 August 2026, a decline of 99.4%.

Mercenary capital is not loyal to the protocol that rents it. It is loyal to the emission.

16.3 Vote-Escrow and the Curve Wars

Curve's answer was to make the incentive itself the object of competition. Locking CRV for up to four years mints veCRV, which is non-transferable, decays linearly with remaining lock time, and confers three things: a share of trading fees, up to a 2.5x boost on the holder's own liquidity mining rewards, and votes over the weekly gauge weights that decide which pools receive CRV emissions.

The last one turned CRV into a market for liquidity. A stablecoin issuer needing deep Curve liquidity could either rent it directly or buy veCRV votes and direct emissions to its own pool. Convex Finance industrialised this by accumulating CRV, locking it permanently, and selling the voting power, and at its peak intermediated a large share of all veCRV. A secondary market emerged in which protocols paid per vote, which is a bribe in the literal and technical sense and is now a standard product.

Curve's TVL peaked at 24.30 billion dollars on 5 January 2022 and stands at 1.34 billion on 30 August 2026. Convex holds 0.56 billion.

The vote-escrow model solved the mercenary capital problem by making the mercenaries owners with a four-year lock. It created a new problem: a governance system whose most valuable asset is the ability to direct someone else's money.

16.4 What a Governance Token Actually Is

Three claims are made for governance tokens, and they hold to different degrees.

A claim on cash flow. Mostly false until recently. Almost no major protocol distributed revenue to holders, largely because doing so invites securities analysis. Uniswap's UNIfication, passed December 2025, works around this by burning UNI with protocol revenue rather than distributing it, and burned 100 million UNI from the treasury retroactively. Aave's treasury accrues the reserve factor and funds a safety module.

A claim on control. True but concentrated. Turnout in most DAOs is low, delegate power is concentrated in a handful of addresses, and specialist risk curators such as Gauntlet and Chaos Labs author most parameter proposals. Whether that is capture or specialisation depends on the reader.

A subsidy for early users. Unambiguously true, and the main historical function. The transfer from token buyers to early users has been very large and is the reason DeFi bootstrapped as fast as it did.

The economics of emission are simple and rarely stated. Emissions are dilution paid to users. A protocol emitting 20% of supply annually to buy 500 million dollars of TVL is paying for that TVL out of every holder's stake. The question is whether the deposit stays when the emission stops, and the historical answer, from SushiSwap and Curve alike, is that it does not.

17.3 The Arithmetic, Step by Step

Step 1: supply. Alice supplies 100 WETH. The Pool mints aWETH with a scaled balance of 100e18 * RAY / liquidityIndex. Her balanceOf reads 100 WETH and grows as the index does. Collateral value is 400,000 dollars.

Step 2: borrowing capacity. At 80.5% LTV she may borrow up to 322,000 dollars. She borrows 240,000 USDC, which is 60% LTV.

Step 3: health factor.

HF = (100 * 4,000 * 0.83) / 240,000 = 332,000 / 240,000 = 1.38333

Step 4: the liquidation price. Solve HF = 1:

100 * P * 0.83 = 240,000   =>   P = 240,000 / 83 = 2,891.57

Alice is liquidatable at a 27.7% drawdown from entry. That is the number she should be watching, and it is not displayed by default in most interfaces.

Step 5: the carry. She pays 4.10% APY on 240,000 dollars, or 9,840 dollars a year, against collateral earning 1.49% on 400,000 dollars, or 5,960 dollars. Net carry is negative 3,880 dollars a year, about 2.4% of her 160,000 dollars of equity and 0.97% of her collateral. Leverage is not free even before anything moves.

Step 6: the drawdown. Ether falls to 2,850 dollars. Collateral is 285,000; health factor is 0.98562.

Step 7: the liquidation. Health factor is above 0.95, so the close factor is 50%. A keeper repays 120,000 USDC and seizes 44.210526 WETH worth 126,000 dollars.

Step 8: what Alice keeps.

Collateral: 100 - 44.210526 = 55.789474 WETH  = $159,000.00
Debt:       240,000 - 120,000                  = $120,000.00
Equity:     159,000 - 120,000                  =  $39,000.00
New HF:     159,000 * 0.83 / 120,000           =    1.09975

Her equity at entry was 160,000 dollars. Ether fell 28.75%. Her equity fell 75.6%. The 6,000 dollar liquidation bonus is 5.0% of that loss; the rest is 2.5x leverage doing what leverage does.

17.4 What Could Have Gone Differently

A gap through the liquidatable band. Between 2,891.57 and 2,400 dollars the position is liquidatable and still solvent. Below 2,400 the collateral is worth less than the debt, and any residual becomes protocol bad debt covered by Aave's Umbrella module or written off.

A liquidation with no bid. At a gas price high enough that the 6,000 dollar bonus does not cover the cost of the transaction plus the unwind, no keeper acts. The bonus scales with position size, so small positions go unliquidated first, which is exactly why Aave raises the close factor to 100% for positions under 2,000 dollars.

E-mode instead. Had Alice supplied wstETH and borrowed WETH in the ETH-correlated e-mode, her LTV would be 93% and her liquidation threshold 95%, with the liquidation bonus at 1%. The buffer between LTV and threshold shrinks from 2.5 points to 2 points, and the maximum looped leverage rises from 5.1x to 14.3x, because the protocol assumes the two prices move together. That assumption is the position's real risk, and it is not in the health factor.

18.3 Four Cases Worth Studying

Euler, 13 March 2023, 197 million dollars. Euler added donateToReserves in a governance upgrade so that users could gift eTokens to the protocol's reserves. The function omitted the health check present on every other balance-reducing path. The attacker flash-borrowed, deposited, minted a heavily leveraged position, then donated enough eTokens to push their own account underwater deliberately. A second contract they controlled then liquidated the first at a discount, acquiring inflated collateral cheaply and withdrawing the underlying. The loss was later returned in full after negotiation, but the lesson stands: adding one function that moves balances without re-running the solvency check invalidates every other check in the system.

Compound, 29 September 2021, 147 million dollars. Proposal 062 upgraded the Comptroller's COMP distribution logic and introduced a comparison that let users claim far more COMP than they had accrued. Anyone could also call drip() on the Reservoir to refill the Comptroller and continue claiming. The bug was not an exploit in the usual sense: users simply clicked "claim." The fix required a governance proposal with its own timelock, so the protocol spent days publicly leaking money it could not stop.

Cetus, 22 May 2025, 223 million dollars. A concentrated liquidity implementation on Sui failed to detect an overflow in the arithmetic that converts a token amount into a liquidity value. An attacker flash-borrowed 56,700 SUI, opened a position spanning about 200 ticks, deposited a single token, and had the broken math credit them with roughly 10^34 units of liquidity, which they then withdrew against real reserves. Sui validators froze about 162 million dollars through emergency coordination; more than 60 million reached Ethereum via a bridge. The technical lesson is about unchecked shifts in a math library. The governance lesson is that a chain whose validators can freeze funds is a chain whose validators can freeze funds.

Balancer, 3 November 2025, 128 million dollars. A scaling helper used mulDown in both directions rather than rounding in whichever direction favoured the pool. Each individual rounding error was a few wei, and a comment in the code recorded that the impact would be minimal. Repeated across thousands of micro-swaps against composable stable pools holding liquid staking derivatives, the error compounded into a drainable gap. Losses spanned Ethereum (24,733 ETH, about 91.5 million dollars), Sonic (3.44 million), Optimism (1.3 million), Arbitrum (1.7 million), Polygon (0.39 million), Base (0.16 million), and Berachain (12.86 million, later recovered by an emergency hard fork). Twenty-seven forks shared the code; copycat attacks started 53 minutes after the first.

Rounding direction is not a style question. It is a solvency invariant.

19.3 The Economics of Each Role

Liquidity providers earn fees and pay loss-versus-rebalancing. Uniswap v4 generated 49.0 million dollars of fees on 25.16 billion dollars of volume over 30 days, an average realised fee of 0.195%; v3 generated 39.8 million on 25.00 billion, or 0.159%. Whether that exceeds LVR is pool-specific and, per the Topaze Blue study of 17 v3 pools, was not true in aggregate over the period measured.

Suppliers earn the borrow rate diluted by utilisation and net of the reserve factor. A USDC supplier on Aave earns 3.31% while borrowers pay 4.10%, a spread of 79 basis points of which the treasury takes 10% of the gross interest.

Borrowers pay interest and, in most cases, a negative carry. Alice in section 17 pays 9,840 dollars a year to earn 5,960 dollars. The trade is leverage, not yield.

Keepers earn the liquidation bonus less gas and unwind cost, in a fully competitive market with no capital barrier. Economic theory says the margin trends to the cost of the marginal keeper, which is gas plus priority fees, and the observed behaviour matches.

Searchers and builders earn the value of ordering. This is a transfer from users and LPs, mediated by a market that pays validators.

The protocol treasury earns the reserve factor, the protocol fee share, or nothing. Uniswap earned nothing at all from v2 and v3 swaps for five years until UNIfication in December 2025.

19.4 What It Costs to Run

A DeFi protocol has almost no marginal cost and a substantial fixed cost, and the fixed cost is not infrastructure.

Deployment gas is a one-off in the low hundreds of thousands of dollars for a complex system on Ethereum mainnet, and far less on an L2.

Audits are the recurring item. A serious protocol runs multiple independent audits at six figures each, plus formal verification, plus a continuing bug bounty. Immunefi bounties for critical smart-contract findings at large protocols reach 10% of funds at risk, capped in the low millions.

Risk management is now an outsourced professional service. Gauntlet, Chaos Labs, Block Analitica, and Steakhouse are paid by DAOs to model parameters and author proposals.

Governance costs real money in delegate compensation, forum operations, and legal wrappers.

Incentives dominate everything else for any protocol still buying growth. A 20% annual emission on a token with a one billion dollar market capitalisation is a 200 million dollar annual expense, whatever the accounting says.

20. Security Engineering and the Defence Stack

20.1 The Layers That Work

Given that 67.4% of losses are operational rather than logical, the defence stack has to start with keys.

Custody and signing. Hardware wallets, m-of-n multisigs with independent signers on independent devices, and transaction simulation before signing. The Bybit loss of 1.40 billion dollars on 21 February 2025 was signer phishing: legitimate signers approved a transaction whose displayed contents did not match its effects. Blind signing is the single largest unmitigated operational risk in the sector.

Timelocks. A governance change that takes effect 48 hours after passing gives the community time to exit. Compound's inability to stop its own COMP leak in September 2021 is the counterexample that proves the value and the cost.

Guardians and pause functions. Aave has an emergency admin that can freeze a reserve. The trade is explicit: a protocol that can be paused has a trusted party, and one that cannot be paused leaks until the exploit finishes.

Caps and ceilings. Supply caps, borrow caps, and isolation-mode debt ceilings bound the loss from any single wrong assumption. This is the most underrated control in DeFi because it converts an unbounded failure into a budgeted one.

Invariant testing and formal verification. Property-based fuzzing that asserts "the sum of scaled balances times the index equals total supply" catches the rounding and accounting bugs that account for 1.263 billion dollars of losses. Certora-style formal verification of the core math is now standard at Aave, Compound, and Morpho.

Bug bounties with real numbers. A bounty must exceed the black-market value of the finding, discounted for the risk of being caught. That is a large number and most bounties are smaller than it.

20.2 The Layers That Do Not

"Audited by" as a badge. Balancer was audited by multiple firms; boosted pools were out of scope in 2023 and the rounding flaw survived to 2025. An audit covers a commit, a scope, and a time budget. It is a snapshot, not a warranty.

Fork provenance. Twenty-seven Balancer forks inherited a bug none of them wrote. Forking audited code inherits the audit only for the code that did not change, which is never the interesting part.

Decentralisation as a security property. A protocol with an upgradeable proxy and a three-of-five multisig is exactly as secure as those three keys, regardless of how many token holders vote.

20.3 The Post-Incident Playbook

The sector has converged on a standard sequence, and its steps are worth naming because they are now expected.

Pause what can be paused. Publish the transaction hashes within hours. Contact the attacker on chain with a bounty offer, typically 10%. Engage chain analytics firms and exchanges to freeze fiat off-ramps. Publish a full post-mortem with the root cause and the code diff. Propose a recapitalisation from the treasury or a token issuance.

Euler recovered nearly all of the 197 million dollars this way. Cetus recovered 162 million with validator cooperation. Balancer recovered 12.86 million on Berachain by hard fork. Most protocols recover nothing.

21. Regulation and Compliance

21.1 The European Union

MiCA, Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, is the first comprehensive crypto framework in a major jurisdiction, and it contains an explicit carve-out that nobody knows how to apply.

Article 149 sets the dates: Titles III and IV, covering asset-referenced and e-money tokens, applied from 30 June 2024; the rest applied from 30 December 2024. Recital 22 states the boundary:

"This Regulation should apply to natural and legal persons and certain other undertakings and to the crypto-asset services and activities performed, provided or controlled, directly or indirectly, by them, including when part of such activities or services is performed in a decentralised manner. Where crypto-asset services are provided in a fully decentralised manner without any intermediary, they should not fall within the scope of this Regulation."

Two words carry the entire regime: "fully decentralised." A protocol with a front end operated by a company, a governance multisig, or a treasury paying developers is not obviously outside. Nobody has tested where the line is, and the incentive structure pushes teams to remove front ends and dissolve entities to stay on the safe side, which is an odd outcome for a consumer protection regulation.

21.2 The United States

US treatment of DeFi has moved sharply since 2024, and the direction is towards accommodation.

The IRS DeFi broker rule was repealed. Treasury finalised regulations in December 2024 that would have treated front-end operators of decentralised exchanges as brokers required to file Forms 1099-DA. Congress disapproved the rule under the Congressional Review Act and the resolution was signed in April 2025, which under the CRA also bars a substantially similar rule.

Stablecoins got a federal framework. The GENIUS Act, signed in July 2025, establishes federal licensing and reserve requirements for payment stablecoin issuers. It regulates the issuer, not the protocol that uses the stablecoin, which leaves DeFi's largest asset class governed and its plumbing not.

Market structure legislation remains unfinished. The Digital Asset Market Clarity Act passed the House in July 2025 and has not been enacted. Until it is, the boundary between a security and a commodity is set by enforcement and litigation rather than statute.

Sanctions law reached a limit. OFAC designated the Tornado Cash smart contracts in August 2022. Litigation established that immutable contracts are not "property" of a sanctioned person under the relevant statute, and Treasury removed the designation in March 2025. The precedent matters far beyond mixers: it constrains the ability to sanction an autonomous contract as opposed to the people who use it.

21.3 What Compliance Looks Like in Practice

Compliance in DeFi happens at the edges, because the middle cannot enforce anything.

Front-end screening. Interfaces geoblock jurisdictions and screen wallet addresses against sanctions lists. The contracts do not.

Permissioned pools. Aave Arc and now Aave Horizon serve institutional participants through whitelisted markets. Horizon holds 375 million dollars across tokenised Treasury and credit collateral on 30 August 2026, with assets such as USTB, JAAA, USCC, VBILL, and JTRSY carrying LTVs from 66% to 88%. This is the shape institutional DeFi actually takes: the same contracts, a gated participant set, and real-world assets as collateral.

Curated vaults. Morpho's MetaMorpho structure lets a named curator select which markets a vault lends into, which reintroduces an identifiable responsible party without touching the underlying protocol.

Travel-rule tooling at the ramps. Fiat on-ramps and off-ramps carry the identity obligations. The protocol layer stays anonymous because it has no mechanism to be anything else.

22.3 When to Use Which

Stable pairs at size: a StableSwap pool or a tight concentrated range. Slippage inside the band is the product.

A volatile pair with no view: full-range v2 or a wide v3 range. Narrow ranges without active management lose to the boundary.

Borrowing against a blue-chip asset: Aave v3, deepest liquidity and the most tested liquidation engine.

Borrowing against something unusual: an isolated Morpho market, where a mistake in that market's oracle cannot reach anyone else.

Executing a large swap: an aggregator or an intent system, not a single pool. Splitting across venues is worth more than any fee tier difference.

23. Modern Developments

23.1 Hooks and Modularity Everywhere

The architectural direction across every category is the same: shrink the immutable core, move opinions into modules.

Uniswap v4 put pool behaviour into hooks. Morpho Blue put risk into curated vaults over immutable markets. Aave v4 put markets into spokes over a shared liquidity hub. Balancer v3 rewrote its vault to host custom pool types. In each case the core got smaller and the periphery got larger, and the audit burden moved with it.

23.2 Real-World Assets and Institutional Rails

Tokenised Treasuries and private credit are now a visible collateral class. BlackRock's BUIDL holds 3.60 billion dollars. Aave Horizon, a permissioned Aave v3 deployment, holds 375 million dollars across tokenised Treasury and credit assets with LTVs of 88% for USTB and VBILL, 86% for JTRSY, 85% for USCC, 81% for JAAA, and 66% for ACRED.

Those LTVs are higher than any crypto asset's, which is the point. A Treasury fund token does not gap 30% in an hour.

23.3 Synthetic Dollar Yield

Ethena's USDe holds 4.08 billion dollars and generated 16.1 million dollars of fees in 30 days. The mechanism is a delta-neutral basis trade: hold spot collateral, short the perpetual, collect funding. It is not overcollateralised lending, not a stablecoin in the reserve sense, and its yield inverts when funding rates go negative. Aave lists USDe with an 8.5% liquidation bonus and a 0% LTV, which is the risk team's opinion made numeric.

23.4 Intents and Order Flow Auctions

The share of DEX volume that never touches a public mempool keeps rising. UniswapX, CoW Protocol, and 1inch Fusion all replace "sign a swap" with "sign a limit order and let solvers compete." The user gets protection from sandwiching; the solver takes the execution risk and whatever spread the competition leaves.

The second-order effect is that the AMM becomes a liquidity source of last resort rather than the venue. Retail order flow gets internalised by professional fillers, and the pool sees only what the fillers could not match. That is a market structure with a familiar name, and it arrived in DeFi in under six years.

23.5 Fee Switches Turn On

Uniswap's UNIfication in December 2025 ended the era in which the largest DEX earned nothing from its own volume. Protocol fees of roughly one sixth of the swap fee now flow to a contract from which value can only be extracted by burning UNI.

Expect the pattern to spread. Every large protocol has a treasury, a token, and a governance body under pressure to justify the token's existence, and burning revenue is the design that survives the most legal scrutiny.

23.6 Where the TVL Went

Total DeFi TVL of 88.69 billion dollars on 30 August 2026 sits half its November 2021 peak, while DEX volume runs at 3.81 trillion dollars a year. The composition changed more than the total. Liquid staking and restaking now hold 30.8 billion between Lido and EigenCloud, more than a third of all TVL, and they are not DeFi in the lending or trading sense at all.

The protocols that actually intermediate risk, the DEXes and money markets, hold a smaller share of a smaller pie and turn it over far faster.

25. Key Takeaways

1. Every AMM price is a stale price, and the LP pays for the update. A constant product pool has no information about the outside world. It learns only when an arbitrageur profits from the difference. That profit is loss-versus-rebalancing, and across 17 Uniswap v3 pools measured in 2021 it exceeded fee income by 60.8 million dollars.

2. Impermanent loss is not impermanent and not a loss on the position. It is 2*sqrt(r)/(1+r) - 1, it depends only on the price ratio, and it is the payoff of a straddle the LP sold. A doubling costs 5.7191%. A tenfold move costs 42.5%.

3. Concentration multiplies both sides of the trade. A plus or minus 10% range is 20.44 times more capital efficient than full range, which means 20 times the fees while in range and roughly 20 times the divergence loss. Out of range it earns nothing at all.

4. Overcollateralised lending is a pawnshop, not credit. It cannot fund anyone who does not already have the money. What it sells is leverage, and the price of that leverage is a negative carry plus a liquidation price the interface does not show.

5. Utilisation is the only input to a lending rate, and the kink is the withdrawal guarantee. USDC on Aave charges 4.10% at 90.09% utilisation and 15.14% at 100%. That gradient across the last eight points is what keeps the pool withdrawable.

6. The liquidation bonus is not a fee, it is a subsidy for a service that must remain profitable under stress. A keeper needs no capital because flash loans exist, and the protocol's solvency depends on the bonus exceeding the unwind cost in the worst market, not the average one. MakerDAO learned this on 12 March 2020 for 5.67 million DAI.

7. Never price collateral from a source the borrower can trade into. Moving a 200 WETH pool by 35.9 times costs 0.75 WETH. That single rule would have prevented 126 recorded incidents and most of the 963 million dollars lost to oracle manipulation.

8. Flash loans do not create vulnerabilities, they remove capital as a control. Only 4 incidents totalling 182.1 million dollars are properly classified as flash loan attacks, all of them governance votes bought for one block. The rest are design flaws that a rich adversary always could have exploited.

9. Composability means inheriting every assumption below you. One rounding direction in Balancer's _upscale cost 128 million dollars across seven chains and 27 forks, with copycats starting 53 minutes in. One Vyper compiler bug disabled reentrancy locks and drained four unrelated protocols in a day.
